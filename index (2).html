QUICK ACADEMY

Your graph-analysis toolkit 📚

A simple strategy: read the chart carefully, notice patterns, then explain what the data shows.

1. Know your charts

These two charts answer different data questions.

📊 Bar chart = compare categories
🥧 Pie chart = show parts of a whole

2. Spot the key information

Check these before you describe anything.

  • Title: What is the chart about?
  • Axes / labels: What is measured?
  • Units: People, %, dollars, or other units?
  • Time period: When was the data collected?

3. Describe trends

increase / rise / grow
decrease / fall / decline
remain stable
fluctuate
reach a peak
hit a low

Example: “The number of participants rose steadily from 20 to 45.”

4. Compare & conclude

higher/lower than
whereas / while
the most / the least
approximately / just over
overall / in contrast

Example: “Overall, music was the most popular club, whereas science was the least popular.”

IELTS LANGUAGE LAB

Language Expressions for IELTS 📖

Build clear, accurate Task 1 descriptions. Choose expressions that fit the data; memorised phrases cannot replace accurate interpretation.

1. Introduction

Paraphrase what the chart illustrates.

The bar chart illustrates the number of…
The bar chart compares the number of… across categories…
The pie charts show the proportion of…

2. Overview

Summarise the main features without listing every number.

Overall, it is clear that…
The most noticeable feature is that…
In general, X accounted for the largest share.

3. Upward & downward trends

X increased steadily from 20 to 45.
The figure rose sharply before levelling off.
There was a gradual decline in…
The figure fluctuated throughout the period.

4. Comparisons & precision

X was considerably higher than Y.
By contrast, the figure for Y remained stable.
The figure peaked at approximately 60%.
X accounted for just under one-third.

5. Numbers & proportions

A rise of 10 units / an increase of 10%
Twice as high as / three times as many as
Approximately / roughly / just over / just under

Remember: “10%” is a percentage; a “10 percentage-point increase” is the difference between two percentages.

6. IELTS response structure

Paragraph 1 — Introduction
Paragraph 2 — Overview
Paragraph 3 — Key details & comparisons

Use data selectively, compare relevant features, and avoid explaining causes unless the chart provides that information.

🎯

Quick speaking / writing challenge

The number of library visits rose from 20 in January to 50 in May.

Model answer: “Overall, library visits increased substantially, rising from 20 in January to 50 in May.”
